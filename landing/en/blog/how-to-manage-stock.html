How to manage stock for small online sellers (without Excel)

If you sell online — through Instagram, WhatsApp, Etsy or Shopify — you've probably experienced this: a customer orders a product, you say "it's in stock", and then you discover you have **only one piece left** that you already promised to another buyer.

Or worse — you run out of your best-seller without even noticing for a week. Customers leave, you lose revenue, and you don't even know why.

In this article, I'll show you **why Excel doesn't work** for online sellers and **what the right way** to manage stock is — without complications and without extra costs.

Why Excel spreadsheets don't work

Excel is great for many things. But for managing an online seller's stock — **it creates more problems than it solves**.

Here's why:

  • Manual updates. Every time you sell something, you need to open Excel and manually reduce the quantity. If you forget — your data is wrong.
  • No alerts. Excel doesn't know how to tell you "this product is running out". You have to notice it yourself.
  • No connection to orders. You track orders in one sheet, stock in another, costs in a third. Everything falls apart when you have more than 20 products.
  • No analytics. You don't know which product brings the most profit and which one loses money.
Excel is for accountants, not for sellers. A seller needs a system that updates itself.

What you actually need

To manage stock without stress, you need a system that:

  1. Automatically reduces stock when you sell a product.
  2. Alerts you when a product falls below the minimum.
  3. Connects orders with stock — one system, one source of truth.
  4. Tracks costs and shows you which product is profitable.
  5. Works on your phone — because a seller is always on the move.

Sounds complicated? It doesn't have to be. There's a solution that does all this automatically — without an IT team.

Practical example: Etsy seller with 30 products

Imagine you sell handmade candles on Etsy. You have 30 different scents, and the best-seller is **the lavender candle** — you sell it 5-6 times a week.

With Excel: Every time you sell, you open Excel, find the row, reduce the quantity. After 2 weeks, you have 15 different versions of the file, you don't know which one is the latest, and your lavender has been selling as "out of stock" for 3 days already.

With MicroStock: When a customer orders lavender, you just enter the order into the system. Stock is **automatically reduced**. If it drops below 5 pieces, you get an **email alert** — "Lavender candle has 4 pieces, time for a new batch".

No Excel. No forgetting. No lost customers.

How to start today

If you currently manage stock in Excel or on paper, here's what I suggest:

  1. List all products — name, SKU (unique code), current quantity, selling and cost price.
  2. Import them into a system — you can do it manually or from CSV if you have them in a table (MicroStock supports CSV import).
  3. Start entering orders — the system will automatically reduce stock.
  4. Set up alerts — for each product, set the minimum quantity below which you want to be notified.

That's it. Within a week, you'll get used to it and **you won't want to go back to Excel**.

Conclusion

Managing stock **doesn't have to be complicated**. Excel was enough when you had 5 products. When you have 20, 50 or 100 — you need a proper tool.
